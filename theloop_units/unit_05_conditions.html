Sometimes Rover-7 needs to check what's actually true right now — is there a wall dead ahead? — and act differently depending on the answer. That's an IF block: "IF this is true, do that."

And real decisions are rarely just one thing. "If it's raining AND I don't have an umbrella, take the bus" checks two things at once before deciding. An IF block can do the same — combine two checks with AND (both must be true) or OR (either one is enough).

📖 Field Manual: CONDITIONS

An IF block only runs its steps when its condition is actually true at that exact moment your program reaches it. Four rules to remember:

  1. It checks its condition each time your program reaches it — not once at the start, and not on a fixed schedule.
  2. If the condition is false, its steps are skipped entirely — Rover-7 just moves on to whatever comes next.
  3. You can combine two checks with AND or OR — AND needs both true at once; OR only needs one of them true.
  4. Sometimes you don't know how many times — only when to stop. A REPEAT UNTIL keeps going, checking its condition before every single pass, until that condition finally becomes true — then it stops, no count needed.

Worked example — climbing until a wall shows up, with no idea how many steps that'll take:

🔄 REPEAT UNTIL Wall Ahead [ ⬆ Up ]

Rover-7 checks for a wall BEFORE every Up. As long as there isn't one, it moves up and checks again. The moment a wall really is ahead, it stops right there — one wall, three, or none, the same block handles all of it.
