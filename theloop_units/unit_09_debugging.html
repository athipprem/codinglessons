The Loop · Unit 09 · Debug Protocol

Every cadet's program breaks sometimes — that's normal, not a failure. What separates a good cadet from a stuck one is DEBUGGING: comparing what a program was SUPPOSED to do against what it ACTUALLY did, finding the exact piece that's wrong, and fixing just that piece. Today you don't build anything new — you're handed two already-written programs that are both broken, and your job is to find out why.

A turn in the wrong direction. That's not a new kind of mistake; it's the MOST common one, and it happens to every real programmer, every single day.

DEBUGGING is the skill of finding out WHY something's wrong, not just noticing that it is. The method is always the same: run it, watch exactly what actually happens, compare that against what SHOULD have happened, and the GAP between the two tells you exactly where to look. Then you fix only that piece — you don't need to rebuild everything from scratch.

📖 Field Manual: DEBUGGING

Three rules for finding a bug fast, instead of guessing:

  1. Run it and watch exactly where it actually stops or goes wrong — not where you assume it does. The real result is always more informative than a guess.
  2. Compare the ACTUAL result against what SHOULD have happened — the size and direction of that gap (1 cell short? Went the wrong way entirely?) points straight at which piece is broken.
  3. Fix only the broken piece — a wrong REPEAT count, a wrong turn direction — using the same Remove / Add / reorder tools you already know. You almost never need to rebuild the whole program.
